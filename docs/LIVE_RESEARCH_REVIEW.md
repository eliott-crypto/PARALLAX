## Reliable points for the demonstration

- **The Verifier found a material constraint on the Bull thesis.** The [official Stagehand FAQ](https://www.browserbase.com/stagehand) confirms an MIT-licensed SDK usable locally without a Browserbase account. The Bull’s withdrawal of _mechanically enforced SDK lock-in_ is justified. Developer preference and a commercial moat remain hypotheses.
- **Identity and the broad career sequence have public support.** [QCon](https://qconsf.com/speakers/paulklein) and [AI Engineer](https://ai.engineer/speakers/paul-klein-iv) identify Paul Klein IV, Browserbase and the Twilio → Stream Club → Mux sequence. [Mux’s acquisition announcement](https://www.mux.com/blog/stream-club-is-joining-mux), dated 22 December 2021, directly identifies Stream Club’s co-founders Lan and Paul. Conference biographies are professional records, not employment audits.
- **Commercial activity has a vendor-published corroboration.** [Stripe’s customer story](https://stripe.com/customers/browserbase) reports more than 100 million usage minutes billed monthly and millions in first-year revenue. This supports billing/adoption at scale; it is a commercial customer story rather than audited financial verification. It discloses neither margins nor Stagehand-driven conversion.
- **Prices have primary support.** [Steel](https://steel.dev/pricing) lists $0.10/$0.08 browser-hour rates; [Browserbase](https://www.browserbase.com/pricing) lists $0.12/$0.10 overage rates after plan allowances. These are different packages with other charges. They establish quoted prices, not realised margins or matched workflow economics.

## Material corrections and limitations

1. **Customer misattribution.** Stored evidence `hunter-25db5414b0d8` and the selected-company strengths incorrectly associate Salesforce and Visa with Browserbase. The cited [Contrary report](https://research.contrary.com/company/browserbase) names them in its **Sauce Labs competitor section**. Browserbase’s customer section names Perplexity, Convergence and Vercel. Do not repeat the misattribution or use customer names as retention proof.

2. **SDK conversion has not been demonstrated.** The Verifier reasons from company revenue to some free-to-paid SDK conversion. Revenue could originate from direct infrastructure signups. The reviewed sources do not establish the acquisition channel or conversion rate. A 2–3% conversion threshold has no sourced economic derivation, and package downloads are not unique users or a valid paid-cohort denominator. Calling the Model Gateway a proven switching-cost mechanism similarly exceeds the evidence; its bundled functionality is real, but retention impact is unmeasured.

3. **Download/date provenance needs qualification.** The [Taskade timeline](https://www.taskade.com/blog/history-of-browserbase) currently displays publication **9 November 2026**, after the audit date, alongside an internal September update. Its stored publication date of 15 October is unsupported. The 5.78M figure is described as **August monthly downloads**, not cumulative downloads; the live outputs use both meanings. The npm API was unavailable to this reviewer, so the historical count was not independently reproduced. [GitHub](https://github.com/browserbase/stagehand) showed approximately 25.7K stars at review time, supporting the broader 25K+ statement only.

4. **Founder claims overreach.** Public solo-founder accounts do not establish that nobody else holds equity or a C-suite position. Exact employment dates remain partly self-reported. The [Okta interview](https://www.okta.com/blog/company-and-culture/founders-in-focus-paul-klein-iv-of-browserbase/) says Stream Club sold in 2020, whereas Mux’s announcement establishes December 2021. Therefore the run’s assertion that no source discrepancies exist is false; use the primary acquisition announcement for that date. Repeated biographies are not five independent employment checks.

5. **Commercial inference is labelled too strongly.** List-price differences do not empirically establish Browserbase margin compression. Self-hosting removes a vendor meter but still costs compute, operations and bandwidth. Stripe billing volume does not contradict a separate task-success benchmark; they measure different things. Vendor/adjacent-provider comparisons from TinyFish, Proxidize and Sendwin should not be treated as neutral benchmark laboratories.

6. **Revenue estimates are historical and heterogeneous.** [GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/browserbase.com) distinguishes a June 2025 $3.6M annual-revenue entry from a July $4.4M **estimate**; it also dates the Series B differently from Browserbase’s own [June announcement](https://www.browserbase.com/blog/series-b-and-beyond). Combining these into a current $4–5M ARR base and a precise valuation multiple overstates comparability. No reviewed source establishes current gross margin, NRR or orchestration attach rates.
